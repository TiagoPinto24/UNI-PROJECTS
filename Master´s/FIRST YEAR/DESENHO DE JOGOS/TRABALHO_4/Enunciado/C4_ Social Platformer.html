C4: Social Platformer

Challenge
Social Platformer (image credits: u/ArtOfBaka)

In this challenge, your goal is to modify a platformer game, making it adequate to social gamers by placing auctions in the core mechanics.

The game must use the theme "Running Out of Power".

Design and Implement a game that:

  • Is derived from a platformer game.
  • The usual platformer mechanics are replaced (or dominated) by auction mechanics to make the game adequate to social gamers .
    • Other social oriented features are allowed but with less influence than auctions.
    • You can keep platformer mechanics, but they must not have great influence in the game outcome.
  • Uses the theme "Running Out of Power".
  • Respects the course contents.

Notice that most platformer games are single-player while social games are... well... social, multi-player.

Resolution

Consider that the core of the game is no longer a platformer, but a social game, featuring auctions. Set polished core mechanics considering the target audience, features, dynamics and the theme.

Think about the player's viewpoint

  • What's this game about?
  • How do I play?
  • How do I win?
  • Why do I want to play?
  • What things do I need to do?

Ensure meaningful decisions

  • Consider the players' options.
  • Choices must affect the game's outcome.
  • Look to keep the players' minds busy.

Apply auction mechanics

  • How do players gather resources to make their bids?
  • What are the items? How do they affect the game outcome?
  • What specific auction types are used?

Deliverable

An archive (a zip file) with:

  • A beta prototype of the game (All systems have been implemented and all code is theoretically complete. No major bugs are expected.).
  • A concept document of the game (a pdf file) as outlined in the Concept Document Structure chapter of the course notes.

Optional Game Features

A Player Skips its Move

Consider that some players will be momentarily unavailable. How can the game progress in that situation?

  • Define some rules that enable the game to continue —so that the other players remain engaged— but without much penalty to the absent player —so that she remains motivated to continue playing.

The Kids Want to Play

Sometimes children want to be part of the adult's activities. Will it be possible to also engage younger players while keeping the adults motivated?

  • Consider "player profiles" that enable "pure luck" progress.

Other Features

Describe and implement other features, not listed here, that might improve the game experience.

Suggested Process

Think about the theme:

  • What is the "power" used to?
  • What is making it "running out"?
  • What are the consequences if it "runs out"?
  • What are the options to avoiding that?

Think about the requirements: how to make a challenge for the player result from to the trade-off in the auctions?

  1. Adapt a platformer game around that theme — you can use a template such as the "2D Platformer - Starter Kit" in Godot's assets library.

  2. Remove the impact that player skill has on the outcome of the game, to make it more accessible to less skilled players — consider, for example, how to exchange skill by luck.

  3. Introduce auction mechanics engaging for social gamers and that support those exchanges.

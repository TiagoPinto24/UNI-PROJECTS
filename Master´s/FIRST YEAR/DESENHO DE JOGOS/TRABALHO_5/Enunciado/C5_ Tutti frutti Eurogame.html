C5: Tutti frutti Eurogame
Detailed view of the board during Terra Mystica gameplay
(By Hubert Figuière from Montréal, Canada)

Challenge

In this challenge you are going to design and implement a game that can be played with easy to find bits/assets: cards, draughts, common game "pawns" and beans. Also, it must be an eurogame (i.e. targeted to the mass market), as defined in the Targeting a Market chapter.

Design and Implement an Eurogame that:

  1. Uses the theme "The more you have the worse it is".
  2. Respects the course contents.
  3. No computer is required to play it — the game bits/assets are exactly the ones listed in the table below (you can get all the assets, and more, in the Kenney Board Game Pack).
Tutti frutti Eurogame Bits/Assets
One common 52-card deck (i.e. the French suit) is the sole source of chance.
One 8x8 draughts board with 2x12 pieces are the "territory" and "terrain/building".
The player's avatars are common player pieces (coloured chess pawns).
Resources are common beans.

Resolution

The game must be playable without the use of a computer. Avoid complex, hard to physically apply mechanics and design simple rules that can be followed "by hand".

This is an eurogame. Define polished core mechanics adequate to that genre restrictions on game setup, considerations about the target audience, play times, etc.

The Player's Viewpoint

  • What's this game about?
  • How do I play?
  • How do I win?
  • Why do I want to play?
  • What things do I need to do?

Meaningful Decisions

  • Consider the players' options.
  • Choices must affect the game's outcome.
  • Look to keep the players' minds busy.

Chance Keeps All Players Engaged

  • How to help lagging behind, but lucky, players having a winning chance?
  • But balanced against strategic, or skillful, players.

The Board

  • What is the role of the "territory" (the draughts board)?
  • Is the color of a "place" having any effect?

The Pieces

  • What is the role of a "terrain/building" piece?
  • Is the color important?
  • Can pieces be stacked? When? Why?
  • Is the initial layout of the pieces pre-determined? Is it random? Is it static?

The Cards

  • What is the role of the suits?
  • What is the role of the rank?
  • How are cards played? Are they given to a player? Drawn from a stack? Discarded?

The Player's Avatars

  • A player will have only one, or many, avatars?
  • Are they static once played or can they move? How?
  • What are the mechanics ruling avatars movement? Is it related to the "terrain/buildings" or other avatars?
  • Can they capture other player's avatars? How?

The Resources

  • How and when are they acquired?
  • What can they be used for? Can they compensate for "bad luck"?

The Theme

  • What is the "thing" that "the more a player has, the worse it is"?
  • Exactly what, and how, is "worse"?
  • How is the player getting that "thing"?
  • What can the player do to get rid of?

Optional Game Features

A Player Skips its Move

Consider that some players will be momentarily unavailable. How can the game progress in that situation?

  • Define some rules that enable the game to continue —so that the other players remain engaged— but without much penalty to the absent player —so that she remains motivated to continue playing.

Other Features

Describe and implement other features, not listed here, that might improve the game experience.

Deliverable

An archive (a zip file) with:

  • A beta prototype of the game (All systems have been implemented and all code is theoretically complete. No major bugs are expected.).
  • A concept document of the game (a pdf file) as outlined in the Concept Document Structure chapter of the course notes.
